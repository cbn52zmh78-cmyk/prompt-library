## I. Composer Profiles

### John Williams — The Wagnerian

**Association:** Spielberg (Jaws, Schindler's List, Saving Private Ryan), Lucas (Star Wars, Indiana Jones)  
**Oscars:** 5 (Fiddler on the Roof, Jaws, Star Wars, E.T., Schindler's List) + 48 additional nominations  
**Philosophy:** Music is the emotional voice of what the camera cannot say. Full orchestral romanticism in the Wagnerian tradition.

**Signature techniques:**
- **Leitmotif architecture** — each major character, location, and concept gets a distinct musical theme. Luke's heroic fanfare, the Imperial March, Hedwig's Theme — these are musical identities as recognizable as faces. Themes evolve across films: the Imperial March first appears triumphant, then becomes funereal.
- **Neoclassical orchestration** — Williams synthesizes Max Steiner's leitmotifs, Alfred Newman's lush string writing, Korngold's heroic brass fanfares, and Herrmann's suspenseful ostinati into a singular orchestral voice. Full symphony orchestra, rarely electronic augmentation.
- **Motivic economy** — Jaws' two-note shark motif is the ultimate example. Simplicity = memorability = dread. The theme works because it's primal — two alternating notes that accelerate, mimicking a heartbeat under threat.
- **Emotional counterpoint** — Schindler's List uses Itzhak Perlman's solo violin against the horror of the visual. The music doesn't match the image — it speaks for the humanity that the image threatens to erase.

**Prompt DNA:** full orchestral leitmotif themes (heroic brass fanfares, yearning string melodies), two-note suspense motifs that accelerate with threat, solo instrument against orchestral mass for emotional counterpoint, Wagnerian romantic sweep

**Pipeline tag:** `music_williams_leitmotif`

### Hans Zimmer — The Architect

**Association:** Nolan (Dark Knight, Inception, Interstellar, Dunkirk, Oppenheimer), Ridley Scott (Gladiator, Black Hawk Down), Denis Villeneuve (Dune)  
**Oscars:** 2 (The Lion King, Dune) + multiple nominations  
**Philosophy:** "Very often, I don't want you to know what is score and sound design." Music should be inseparable from the sonic architecture of the film.

**Signature techniques:**
- **Electronic-orchestral hybrid** — modular synthesizers sit alongside 100-piece orchestras. The Dark Knight is a 50/50 split between live orchestra and electronic sound design. Neither dominates — they're one texture.
- **Non-musical elements as score** — Dunkirk uses the ticking of a real pocket watch as the rhythmic backbone. Interstellar uses church organ as architecture, not decoration. Inception's "BRAAAM" is four wind instruments (bassoon, French horn, trombone, tuba) playing one note simultaneously, processed into seismic weight.
- **Shepard tone integration** — Dunkirk's score uses the Shepard tone (an auditory illusion of infinitely rising pitch) throughout, creating a sense of mounting, unresolvable tension. The audience feels the dread but can't identify why.
- **Motoric pulse** — rhythmic, driving patterns that carry physical momentum. The Dark Knight's Joker theme is essentially one note on a cello, bowed with increasing pressure. Tension through texture, not melody.

**Prompt DNA:** electronic-orchestral hybrid (synth pads under live strings), non-musical elements as rhythmic backbone (ticking, machinery), Shepard tone tension builds, motoric single-note cello/bass drone with increasing pressure, seismic low-end brass hits

**Pipeline tag:** `music_zimmer_architect`

### Bernard Herrmann — The Suspensionist

**Association:** Hitchcock (Psycho, Vertigo, North by Northwest), Scorsese (Taxi Driver, Cape Fear)  
**Philosophy:** Music should express the emotional fluctuation of characters, not illustrate physical actions. Herrmann was the first to abandon illustrative scoring for psychological scoring.

**Signature techniques:**
- **Constrained orchestration** — Psycho uses *only* string instruments. No brass, no winds, no percussion. This constraint forced Herrmann to push strings to their absolute limits — shrieking glissandi, pizzicato stabs, tremolo terror. The single palette mirrors the black-and-white film stock.
- **Cluster chord construction** — the shower scene builds a chord from E, Eb, F, Gb — notes a semitone apart stacked into maximum dissonance. Each string section enters in descending pitch, building the cluster. This technique became the template for every horror stinger that followed.
- **Emotional pre-cognition** — Herrmann's music tells you how to feel before you know why. Vertigo's spiral motif creates dizziness through descending chromatic patterns before the camera reveals the height.
- **Ostinato as anxiety** — repeating rhythmic cells that create unresolvable tension. The audience waits for resolution that never comes.

**Prompt DNA:** string-only constrained orchestration, dissonant semitone cluster chords, shrieking high-register string stabs (Psycho-style stingers), descending chromatic spirals for vertigo/dread, unresolved ostinato patterns

**Pipeline tag:** `music_herrmann_suspense`

### Ennio Morricone — The Melodist

**Association:** Sergio Leone (Dollars Trilogy, Once Upon a Time in the West/America), Tarantino (Hateful Eight), Tornatore (Cinema Paradiso)  
**Oscars:** 1 (Hateful Eight) + Honorary Oscar  
**Philosophy:** A film score should be singable. If you can't whistle the theme walking out of the theater, the music failed.

**Signature techniques:**
- **Character-instrument leitmotifs** — in The Good, the Bad and the Ugly, each character has not just a theme but a *specific instrument*: flute for Blondie, ocarina for Angel Eyes, human voices for Tuco. The instrument IS the character.
- **Unorthodox instrumentation** — electric guitars in westerns (unheard-of in 1964), whistling, cracking whips, gunshots, animal cries integrated as musical elements. He treated sound effects as instruments and instruments as sound effects.
- **Emotional architecture through crescendo** — "The Ecstasy of Gold" begins with a solo cor anglais floating over piano, then adds soprano voice, then strings, then full orchestra in a 3-minute crescendo that mirrors Tuco's escalating mania. The music IS the character's emotional arc made audible.
- **Avant-garde meets folk melody** — trained as a classical avant-gardist, Morricone brought dissonance, aleatoric techniques, and extended instrumental methods into scores that simultaneously featured the most hummable melodies in cinema history.

**Prompt DNA:** character-instrument assignment (specific instruments for specific characters), folk melody over avant-garde harmonic bed, slow-build crescendo matching emotional arc, unorthodox instrumentation (whistling, whips, electric guitar in period settings), singable themes that lodge permanently

**Pipeline tag:** `music_morricone_melodist`

### Trent Reznor & Atticus Ross — The Hybridists

**Association:** David Fincher (Social Network, Gone Girl, Mank), Luca Guadagnino (Challengers, Queer)  
**Oscars:** 2 (Social Network, Soul)  
**Philosophy:** The score IS the sound design. No boundary between music and ambient texture.

*Covered in depth in `cinematic_sound_design_deep_research_v1.md` — included here for completeness as they sit at the exact intersection of sound design and scoring.*

**Signature techniques:**
- **Whole-film-first composition** — compose entire albums of material before seeing a rough cut. The director selects pieces. This gives the score cohesive identity rather than scene-by-scene reactivity.
- **Weaponized beauty** — in Gone Girl, serene piano becomes increasingly sinister through narrative context alone. The music doesn't change — your understanding of it does.
- **Digital artifacts as emotion** — static, glitch, hiss aren't errors. They're emotional content. The Social Network's score represents code's cold precision through digital texture.
- **Industrial-ambient scoring** — Nine Inch Nails' background fused with film composition. Modular synths alongside string quartets. The listener can't locate where "music" ends and "sound" begins.

**Prompt DNA:** electronic-ambient score blurring music/sound-design boundary, beautiful melodies that become sinister through context, digital artifacts (glitch, static, hiss) as emotional content, modular synth drones under acoustic instruments, whole-album compositional identity

**Pipeline tag:** `music_reznor_ross` (already proposed as `sound_reznor_ross` in sound design pass — same tag serves both since their work erases the distinction)

### Hildur Gudnadottir — The Instrument Builder

### Jonny Greenwood — The Dissonant

**Association:** Paul Thomas Anderson (There Will Be Blood, Phantom Thread, The Master, Licorice Pizza)  
**Philosophy:** Classical dissonance and controlled chaos. Rock musician's instinct for texture applied to orchestral forces.

**Signature techniques:**
- **Twelve-tone cluster writing** — There Will Be Blood opens with a chord of twelve chromatic notes played by strings, each sliding toward F-sharp on different trajectories. The result is a sound of the earth itself — geological, primordial, unsettled.
- **Aleatoric composition** — Greenwood gives musicians instructions with deliberate randomness: different players assigned peculiar trills subject to chance, creating textures that are orchestrated chaos. Inspired by Penderecki's *Threnody to the Victims of Hiroshima*.
- **Beauty-to-violence whiplash** — Phantom Thread luxuriates in classical beauty, then jazz and dissonance crack through like neurotic fissures. The music mirrors Reynolds Woodcock's personality — refined surface, bristling underneath.
- **Materiality of sound** — Greenwood composes for the physical act of playing. Bow pressure, string tension, breath pressure — the effort of making the sound is part of the sound's meaning.

**Prompt DNA:** twelve-tone chromatic clusters sliding toward resolution, aleatoric orchestral textures (controlled randomness), beauty interrupted by dissonant cracks, physicality of instrumental performance audible in the score, Penderecki-influenced string writing

**Pipeline tag:** `music_greenwood_dissonant`

### Ryuichi Sakamoto — The Boundary Crosser

**Association:** Nagisa Oshima (Merry Christmas, Mr. Lawrence), Bertolucci (The Last Emperor), Iñárritu (The Revenant)  
**Oscars:** 1 (The Last Emperor) + Grammy, BAFTA  
**Philosophy:** Music has no cultural borders. Classical, electronic, traditional Japanese, African percussion, minimalism — all are one palette.

**Signature techniques:**
- **Cultural code-switching** — The Last Emperor seamlessly integrates traditional Chinese instruments with Western orchestral writing. Neither tradition dominates; they inform each other.
- **Minimalist-electronic foundation** — trained in the YMO (Yellow Magic Orchestra) electronic tradition, Sakamoto builds scores from repetitive minimalist cells (influenced by Feldman and Cage) that evolve through subtle harmonic shifts.
- **Organic-electronic tension** — The Revenant combines taut, minimal electronic textures (with collaborator Alva Noto) alongside organic sounds that intensify the film's raw elemental nature. Nature and technology coexist uneasily.
- **Image album method** — like Hisaishi, Sakamoto composes an "image album" early in production that directors listen to while shooting/editing. The music shapes the film's rhythm before the final cut exists.

**Prompt DNA:** cross-cultural instrumentation (Western orchestra + traditional instruments from the depicted culture), minimalist electronic cells evolving through subtle harmonic shifts, organic-electronic coexistence, spare piano motifs with electronic texture beneath

**Pipeline tag:** `music_sakamoto_crosscultural`

### Joe Hisaishi — The Dreamer

**Association:** Hayao Miyazaki / Studio Ghibli (Spirited Away, Princess Mononoke, Howl's Moving Castle, My Neighbor Totoro)  
**Philosophy:** Start with one melody. That melody is the emotional spine. Everything else serves it.

**Signature techniques:**
- **Central melody method** — every Ghibli score begins with one strong central melody, then reuses it in inventive variations throughout. The theme for "One Summer's Day" (Spirited Away) appears as piano solo, full orchestral sweep, and stripped-back celesta across the film. Same DNA, different emotional temperatures.
- **Image album workflow** — Hisaishi composes music from character descriptions and concept art *before animation begins*. Miyazaki then listens to this music while working, and the animation's rhythm absorbs the score's tempo. The music doesn't follow the film — the film follows the music.
- **Impressionist orchestration** — drawing on Debussy and Ravel, Hisaishi favors shimmering string textures, harp arpeggios, and woodwind colors that evoke wonder and melancholy simultaneously. The orchestra sounds like watercolor.
- **Innocence without simplicity** — the melodies sound childlike but are harmonically sophisticated. Princess Mononoke's theme uses a full orchestra and choir with power that rivals any Wagner, but the emotional register remains intimate.

**Prompt DNA:** single central melody varied across emotional temperatures (piano solo → full orchestra → music box), Impressionist shimmering strings and harp, wonder-and-melancholy coexistence, choir with orchestral power at intimate emotional scale, Debussy-influenced watercolor orchestration

**Pipeline tag:** `music_hisaishi_dreamer`

### Nino Rota — The Storyteller

**Association:** Federico Fellini (La Dolce Vita, 8½, Amarcord), Coppola (The Godfather I & II)  
**Oscars:** 1 (The Godfather Part II)  
**Philosophy:** Music should be the unifying force of a film. When the director's vision is fragmentary, the score holds it together.

**Signature techniques:**
- **Circus-waltz duality** — Fellini's films live in a space between carnival and melancholy. Rota's scores mix dance tunes, circus marches, and American standards with waltzes that carry deep sadness underneath their surface charm. The music smiles while the story weeps.
- **Melodic unity across fragmentation** — Fellini's films are episodic, dreamlike, non-linear. Rota's recurring themes are the narrative thread the audience holds onto. The score provides continuity that the editing deliberately withholds.
- **Forlorn monophony** — The Godfather opens with a solo trumpet playing a monophonic theme that blossoms into the Godfather Waltz. Starting with a single unaccompanied voice creates intimacy and portent simultaneously.
- **Eclecticism as identity** — classical, jazz, circus music, Italian folk — Rota blends everything into a sound that is unmistakably his. The eclecticism isn't pastiche; it's a worldview where high and low culture coexist naturally.

**Prompt DNA:** waltz rhythms carrying melancholy beneath charm, solo trumpet/instrument opening into full theme (monophony → polyphony), circus-carnival motifs with emotional undercurrent, folk-classical-jazz eclecticism as unified voice, recurring melodic threads across episodic structure

**Pipeline tag:** `music_rota_storyteller`

### Ludwig Göransson — The Fusionist

**Association:** Ryan Coogler (Black Panther, Creed, Sinners), Christopher Nolan (Tenet, Oppenheimer)  
**Oscars:** 3 (Black Panther, Oppenheimer, Sinners)  
**Philosophy:** Every film demands its own instrument. Travel to the source culture, find authentic sounds, then fuse them with electronics and orchestra.

**Signature techniques:**
- **Field research as composition** — for Black Panther, Göransson traveled to the International Library of African Music in South Africa, listening to traditional recordings to build a culturally authentic sonic vocabulary before writing a note. The research IS the composition process.
- **Palindromic composition** — Tenet's score sounds the same forwards and in reverse, mirroring the film's temporal inversion concept. Melodies are composed as palindromes, creating an eerie sense of temporal ambiguity.
- **Era-specific sampling** — for Oppenheimer, Göransson had a sound specialist record actual 1950s-era equipment (radios, nuclear computers, machines) then fed those recordings into a modular synthesizer to create a sample library. The score's texture is literally made of the film's era.
- **Genre destruction** — refuses to stay in one lane. Hip-hop production for Creed, African instrumentation for Black Panther, palindromic electronics for Tenet, violin + synth for Oppenheimer. Every film gets a custom sonic identity built from the ground up.

**Prompt DNA:** culturally authentic instrumentation fused with electronic processing, palindromic/reversible musical phrases, era-specific sampled textures through modular synth, genre-defying fusion (orchestral + electronic + cultural source instruments), field-research-driven authenticity

**Pipeline tag:** `music_goransson_fusion`

### Alexandre Desplat — The Impressionist

**Association:** Wes Anderson (Grand Budapest Hotel, Isle of Dogs, French Dispatch), Guillermo del Toro (Shape of Water)  
**Oscars:** 2 (Grand Budapest Hotel, Shape of Water)  
**Philosophy:** The orchestra is a resonant chamber, an extension of the human body. Compose scenes, not cues.

**Signature techniques:**
- **Cultural-instrumental specificity** — Grand Budapest Hotel's "Mittel-Europa" sound uses zithers, balalaikas (35-player balalaika orchestra), and alpenhorns. The score sounds like a mythical place that is "vaguely European" — specific enough to feel real, generic enough to feel fictional.
- **Aquatic orchestration** — for Shape of Water, Desplat made the orchestration feel "as though we are surrounded by water." Instruments are voiced to create a sense of liquid immersion — flowing, connected, without hard edges.
- **Musical memory** — Desplat's music remembers. It returns to earlier material with variations, as if reflecting on what has passed. Themes evolve through the film as the characters do.
- **Chamber-scale intimacy** — favors strings, harp, celesta, and close-voiced winds. Even in full orchestral passages, Desplat's writing retains chamber music intimacy. The orchestra breathes like a small ensemble.

**Prompt DNA:** culturally specific instrumentation for fictional settings (balalaika, zither, alpenhorn for "vaguely European"), aquatic flowing orchestration without hard edges, chamber-intimate voicing within full orchestra, musical themes that return in varied form as memory/reflection, harp-celesta-string warmth

**Pipeline tag:** `music_desplat_impressionist`

### Carter Burwell — The Minimalist Partner

**Association:** Coen Brothers (Blood Simple through Hail Caesar), Spike Jonze (Being John Malkovich, Her), Todd Haynes (Carol)  
**Philosophy:** The best film score is the one you barely notice but deeply feel. Music should not assert itself — it should dissolve into the film.

**Signature techniques:**
- **Post-minimalist lyricism** — spare, repeating musical cells that create emotion through accumulation rather than melody. Influenced by Steve Reich and Philip Glass, but warmer and more narratively responsive.
- **Sound design blur** — Burwell works closely with Skip Lievsay (sound designer) on Coen films to blur the boundary between music and environmental sound. Where does the score end and the wind begin?
- **Unorthodox instrument selection** — Fargo uses a Hardanger fiddle (Norwegian instrument with two sets of strings, one resonating sympathetically). Blood Simple uses "ominously-spare piano" with "electronic synth texture that buzzes with sharpness and malice." Each film gets its own sonic personality through one unusual instrument choice.
- **Negative-space scoring** — many Coen films have almost no score (No Country for Old Men has roughly 16 minutes of music in a 2-hour film). Burwell's restraint makes every note count.

**Prompt DNA:** post-minimalist repeating cells building through accumulation, music-to-sound-design dissolution (score becomes environment), one unusual instrument defining the sonic world (Hardanger fiddle, prepared piano), extreme restraint (negative-space scoring), spare piano with electronic undertone

**Pipeline tag:** `music_burwell_minimalist`

---

### A.R. Rahman — The Alchemist

**Association:** Danny Boyle (Slumdog Millionaire, 127 Hours), Mani Ratnam (Roja, Bombay), plus 100+ Indian films  
**Oscars:** 2 (Slumdog Millionaire — Score + Song)  
**Philosophy:** Music has no geographic border. Indian classical, electronica, Sufi devotional, Western orchestral — fuse them all into one voice that anyone can feel.

**Signature techniques:**
- **Multicultural fusion as native language** — Rahman doesn't "add" Indian elements to Western scores or vice versa. His natural compositional voice is the fusion itself. Slumdog Millionaire mixes Indian classical, bhangra, reggae, Brazilian drumming, and Western electronica as one unified texture.
- **Technology as instrument** — from his earliest Tamil scores (Roja, 1992), Rahman integrated samplers and synthesizers alongside traditional instruments. Technology isn't a substitute — it's another instrument in the ensemble.
- **Raag-based harmonic structure** — The Bombay Theme uses Raag Jajaivanti as its harmonic foundation. Rahman maps Indian raga structures onto Western compositional forms, creating harmony that feels universal but carries centuries of Indian musical theory.
- **Lush strings + electronic bass** — his signature texture: warm, expansive string writing married with computer-generated bass frequencies. Organic on top, synthetic underneath.

**Prompt DNA:** multicultural fusion (Indian classical + electronic + world percussion as unified voice), raag-based harmonic structure beneath Western orchestration, lush strings over synthesized bass, Sufi/devotional vocal textures, technology and tradition as equal instruments

**Pipeline tag:** `music_rahman_fusion`

## II. Scoring Technique Taxonomy

### Structural Techniques

| Technique | Definition | Prompt Language |
|-----------|-----------|-----------------|
| **Leitmotif** | Recurring theme assigned to a character, concept, or place — evolves as they do (Wagner → Williams) | "leitmotif theme for [character] — recurring melodic identity that transforms with narrative arc" |
| **Mickey Mousing** | Music that directly mimics on-screen movement, note-for-note (early Disney, comedic scoring) | "mickey-mousing sync — music hits precisely matching on-screen physical actions" |
| **Underscore** | Background music supporting mood without drawing attention to itself | "atmospheric underscore — subtle emotional support beneath dialogue" |
| **Source scoring** | Music that begins as diegetic (a radio, a performer) and transitions seamlessly into non-diegetic score | "source-to-score crossfade — diegetic music source dissolves into orchestral underscore" |
| **Temp track influence** | Using pre-existing music during editing to establish mood, which the final score must match or deliberately depart from | *(production concept, not prompt-injectable)* |

### Compositional Techniques

| Technique | Definition | Prompt Language |
|-----------|-----------|-----------------|
| **Ostinato** | Repeating rhythmic/melodic pattern creating tension through persistence (Herrmann, Zimmer, Carpenter) | "ostinato tension build — repeating rhythmic cell that refuses to resolve" |
| **Pedal point** | Sustained bass note while harmony changes above it, creating grounding tension | "pedal point anchor — sustained bass drone under shifting harmonic movement" |
| **Counterpoint (ironic)** | Music that deliberately contradicts the emotional content of the image (Kubrick's "We'll Meet Again" over nuclear explosions) | "ironic musical counterpoint — cheerful/beautiful music against dark imagery" |
| **Cluster chord** | Multiple adjacent notes sounded simultaneously for maximum dissonance (Herrmann, Greenwood, Penderecki) | "dissonant cluster chord — chromatic notes stacked for maximum harmonic tension" |
| **Aleatoric composition** | Elements of controlled randomness in orchestral performance (Greenwood, Penderecki) | "aleatoric orchestral texture — controlled randomness across string sections" |
| **Palindromic composition** | Music that sounds the same forwards and backwards (Göransson/Tenet) | "palindromic musical phrase — melody that reads identically in reverse" |

### Orchestration Techniques

| Technique | Definition | Prompt Language |
|-----------|-----------|-----------------|
| **Constrained orchestration** | Using only one section of the orchestra (Herrmann's strings-only Psycho) | "constrained [strings-only/brass-only] orchestration — single family pushed to extremes" |
| **Cross-cultural instrumentation** | Integrating instruments from the depicted culture alongside Western orchestra (Göransson, Sakamoto, Rahman) | "cross-cultural instrumentation — [specific instruments] integrated with orchestral forces" |
| **Chamber-scale intimacy** | Writing for full orchestra but maintaining the character of a small ensemble (Desplat) | "chamber-intimate orchestration — full orchestra voiced with small-ensemble clarity" |
| **Electronic-orchestral hybrid** | Seamless blend of synthesizers/electronics with live orchestra (Zimmer, Reznor/Ross, Sakamoto) | "electronic-orchestral hybrid — synth textures and live instruments as one voice" |
| **Monophony → polyphony** | Beginning with a single unaccompanied line, then building into full ensemble (Morricone, Rota) | "monophonic opening — single instrument building to full orchestral statement" |

### Emotional/Narrative Techniques

| Technique | Definition | Prompt Language |
|-----------|-----------|-----------------|
| **Emotional pre-cognition** | Music that tells the audience how to feel before the narrative reason is revealed (Herrmann) | "emotional foreshadowing — score signals dread/wonder before the visual reveal" |
| **Weaponized beauty** | Beautiful music that becomes sinister through narrative recontextualization (Reznor/Ross) | "weaponized beauty — serene melody acquiring menace through story context" |
| **Negative-space scoring** | Deliberate extreme restraint — most of the film has no score (Burwell/Coens, Lievsay) | "negative-space score — music absent for long stretches, making every note seismic" |
| **Score-shapes-performance** | Music composed before shooting, played on set to influence actors' physical performance (Gudnadottir/Joker, Hisaishi/Miyazaki) | *(production concept — relevant to script construction, not prompt injection)* |
| **Musical memory** | Themes returning in varied form, evoking what the audience felt when they first heard them (Desplat, Williams) | "musical memory callback — earlier theme returning in new orchestration, carrying accumulated emotion" |

### Production Workflow Terms

| Term | Definition | Pipeline Relevance |
|------|-----------|-------------------|
| **Spotting session** | Director + composer watch the locked picture together, deciding where music starts/stops and what it should convey | Equivalent: script JSON beat structure — `narration: true/false` and `audio_dna_tag` assignment |
| **Cue** | A single continuous piece of score, from start point to end point | Equivalent: one block in the branch-chain or one shot in longform |
| **Cue sheet** | Document listing every piece of music in the film with timing, source, and rights info | Equivalent: script JSON's `config.audio_dna_tag` + per-shot overrides |
| **Dubbing** | Final mix where score, dialogue, and sound effects are combined | Equivalent: Grok's native AV pass (simultaneous) + post-stitch EQ |
| **Temp track** | Pre-existing music used during editing to establish mood before the real score is composed | Equivalent: `style_dna_tag` + `audio_dna_tag` in script JSON — they're the "temp" that becomes the production sound |

## III. Proposed Music Tags for Pipeline

### New `music_*` tag set (10 tags)

Unlike the `sound_*` tags which map 1:1 to visual `style_*` pairs, these `music_*` tags are composer-centric and style-independent. A script could combine any visual tag with any music tag — `style_fincher_khondji` visual + `music_morricone_melodist` score is a valid (and interesting) combination.

| Tag ID | Anchor Composer | Sonic Character | Best For |
|--------|----------------|-----------------|----------|
| `music_williams_leitmotif` | John Williams | Full orchestral, heroic brass, yearning strings, memorable themes | Epic narrative, character-driven stories, adventure, emotional drama |
| `music_zimmer_architect` | Hans Zimmer | Electronic-orchestral hybrid, seismic bass, motoric pulse, Shepard tones | Large-scale spectacle, psychological tension, sci-fi, war |
| `music_herrmann_suspense` | Bernard Herrmann | Strings-only, cluster chords, shrieking stingers, unresolved ostinato | Horror, thriller, psychological suspense, Hitchcockian tension |
| `music_morricone_melodist` | Ennio Morricone | Character-instrument assignment, folk melody, slow-build crescendo | Western, period drama, epic sweep, character-driven narrative |
| `music_greenwood_dissonant` | Jonny Greenwood | Twelve-tone clusters, aleatoric textures, beauty-violence whiplash | Art-house drama, psychological intensity, controlled chaos |
| `music_sakamoto_crosscultural` | Ryuichi Sakamoto | Cross-cultural instrumentation, minimalist electronic cells, organic-electronic coexistence | Period pieces across cultures, contemplative drama, nature vs. technology |
| `music_hisaishi_dreamer` | Joe Hisaishi | Single central melody varied, Impressionist shimmer, wonder-melancholy coexistence | Animation, fantasy, coming-of-age, contemplative beauty |
| `music_rota_storyteller` | Nino Rota | Waltz-melancholy, circus-carnival motifs, solo-to-full-ensemble builds | Italian drama, episodic narrative, tragicomedy, period elegance |
| `music_goransson_fusion` | Ludwig Göransson | Field-research authenticity, palindromic phrases, era-specific sampling, genre destruction | Contemporary action, culturally specific settings, temporal narratives |
| `music_desplat_impressionist` | Alexandre Desplat | Chamber-intimate full orchestra, aquatic flow, culturally specific fictional worlds | Period whimsy, romantic drama, fairy-tale realism |

### Relationship to existing `sound_*` tags

The `music_*` and `sound_*` tag sets are independent and combinable. A script declares both:

```json
{
  "config": {
    "style_dna_tag": "hybrid_fincher_deakins",
    "audio_dna_tag": "sound_fincher_khondji",
    "music_dna_tag": "music_reznor_ross"
  }
}
```

For Grok's native AV pass (which generates score + SFX + dialogue simultaneously), the resolver would merge the `sound_*` and `music_*` prompt fragments into one unified audio directive. The `sound_*` tag governs effects/ambience/foley, the `music_*` tag governs score/melody/harmony.

When no `music_dna_tag` is specified, the existing `audio_dna_tag` handles everything (backward compatible).
